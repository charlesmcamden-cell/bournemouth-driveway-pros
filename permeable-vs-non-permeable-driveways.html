Permeable vs Non-Permeable Driveways: What You Need to Know

Published 27 August 2026 · Bournemouth Driveway Pros Guides

Quick answer: A non-permeable driveway larger than 5m² needs planning permission unless rainwater drains to a permeable area like a lawn or border. Permeable surfaces — gravel, permeable block paving, or resin bound with a SUDS-compliant base — are exempt from this rule regardless of size.

What Counts as Permeable

Gravel is naturally permeable. Permeable block paving uses gapped joints and a porous sub-base to let water through. Resin bound, laid on the correct porous base, is also fully permeable. Standard tarmac and standard block paving are not.

Why It Matters for Planning Permission

Under permitted development rules, a non-permeable driveway over 5m² needs planning permission unless the water is directed to a permeable area of your own garden rather than the street. See our full planning permission guide for the details.

Choosing the Right Option

If avoiding planning permission matters to you, a permeable material is the simplest route — and has the added benefit of reducing surface flooding risk on your property and the wider street.
